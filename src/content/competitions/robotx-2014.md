## Overview

The first Maritime RobotX Challenge was staged at Marina Bay on Singapore's southern
shore — a sheltered course with enough public visibility to draw spectators. Flinders
University entered alongside the Australian Maritime College, bringing TopCat to its
first international event.

## The course

Vessels ran a set of tasks fully autonomously: navigating a buoy field, identifying and
docking against marked bays, and localising an underwater acoustic beacon. Scoring
combined the number of tasks completed, positional accuracy, and time taken.

## How it went

TopCat reached Singapore with very little on-water testing behind it, so a large share
of systems integration and debugging was done in the pits between runs. The team was
given a special mention for its use of the vessel as a research platform.

## Lessons learnt

- **Field testing is the whole game.** A design that is sound on paper still has to be
  proven on the water, and there is no substitute for hours of it.
- **The environment attacks the hardware.** Heat and humidity fogged camera housings and
  pushed onboard computers into thermal throttling — neither had shown up in Adelaide.
- **Check the numbers, then check them again.** Battery placement had to be reworked
  mid-competition after trim came out wrong.
